Eurovision 2020: the contest that never happened

Eurovision 2020 was called off two months before Rotterdam. Here’s what happened to its 41 songs, including the one that went viral anyway.

Updated December 14, 2026 · 5 min read

The short answer
  • The EBU canceled it on March 18, 2020, because of COVID-19
  • It’s the only year since 1956 without a contest
  • Iceland’s “Think About Things” became the breakout hit anyway
  • Rotterdam hosted in 2021, and 26 of the 2020 acts came back with new songs

What happened

The 65th contest was set for Rotterdam Ahoy, with semi-finals on May 12 and 14, 2020, and the Grand Final on May 16. The Netherlands was hosting because Duncan Laurence had won in Tel Aviv with “Arcade,” and the slogan was “Open Up.” Forty-one countries signed up.

On March 18, the EBU called it off, citing the uncertainty around COVID-19. Postponing and holding the show without an audience were both considered and ruled out. It’s the only year since 1956 without a Eurovision, and Ahoy itself was later turned into a temporary care facility for patients.

Two days later came the decision that stung the most. The rules bar any song released before September 1 of the year before the contest, so none of the 2020 songs could compete in 2021. Any act that wanted to come back needed a new song.

The show that replaced it

On May 16, the night the Grand Final would have aired, the hosts picked for Rotterdam presented Eurovision: Europe Shine a Light from a studio in Hilversum. They introduced a short clip of every 2020 song, and past winners dropped in, from Johnny Logan to Netta. Nobody won.

It ended with almost every 2020 act singing “Love Shine a Light,” the UK’s 1997 winner, each from their own country. The EBU counted 73 million viewers.

Think About Things

Daði Freyr and his band Gagnamagnið won Iceland’s national selection with a song Daði wrote for his baby daughter. The video, with the band in matching green sweaters, took off in February. Russell Crowe shared it with a one-word tweet: “Song.”

The cancellation turned it into the unofficial winner of a contest that never took place. It topped the stand-in votes in Sweden, Norway, Austria and Australia, and it reached No. 34 in the UK, the first Eurovision entry in the UK Top 40 since “Heroes” in 2015. Jennifer Garner posted her own take on the dance.

In 2021 the band came back with “10 Years” and finished fourth without performing live. A band member tested positive for COVID-19 in Rotterdam, so the semi-final and the final both aired their rehearsal from May 13.

The stand-in contests

With nothing to vote in, broadcasters ran their own shows. Germany’s went to Lithuania’s The Roop and “On Fire,” which beat “Think About Things” 22 points to 19, and the fan clubs’ OGAE poll also put Lithuania first. The BBC left the 2020 songs out and had viewers pick a favorite from past UK entries.

Fans started their own tradition too. #EurovisionAgain rewatched an old Grand Final together every week and raised money for charity along the way.

Fire Saga

America’s biggest Eurovision moment that year came from Netflix. Eurovision Song Contest: The Story of Fire Saga, with Will Ferrell and Rachel McAdams as an Icelandic duo, was meant to come out alongside the contest. It arrived on June 26 instead. Loreen and Netta are among the Eurovision cameos, and “Husavik” was nominated for the Oscar for Best Original Song.

That August, Eurovision’s producers also announced the American Song Contest. It finally aired on NBC in 2022.

Rotterdam, one year later

Rotterdam got its contest in May 2021, with 3,500 people allowed in the arena for each show. Every country also recorded a backup performance at home in case its act couldn’t travel or had to quarantine.

Twenty-six of the 2020 acts came back with new songs. Switzerland’s Gjon’s Tears finished third and Ukraine’s Go_A fifth. Italy won with Måneskin’s “Zitti e buoni,” which we put to the five tests in Episode 05.

Where to watch

Clips from Europe Shine a Light are on eurovision.tv, and the 2020 music videos are on the official Eurovision Song Contest YouTube channel. Fire Saga is on Netflix. Our episode on “Think About Things” is Episode 09.
